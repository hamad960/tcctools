How to make a scatter plot for a scientific paper

Updated October 1, 2026 · 8 min read · By tcctools Editorial Team

Few details undermine an otherwise strong manuscript as quickly as blurry figures, clipped axis titles, or unreadable color combinations. In quantitative research, the scatter plot is the primary visualization tool to demonstrate relationships between two continuous variables.

1. The 300 DPI standard

Computer screens typically display graphics at 72 to 96 dots per inch (DPI). However, academic journals require a minimum of 300 DPI for color/grayscale raster images and up to 600–1200 DPI for pure line art. Taking a desktop screenshot of your software will produce pixelated figures in the final publication PDF. Always export raw vector formats (PDF/SVG) or high-DPI rasters.

2. Anatomy of a publication figure

  • X-axis (Horizontal): Place the independent (predictor) variable here. Always include variable name and measurement units in parentheses: Administered Dose (mg/kg).
  • Y-axis (Vertical): Place the dependent (outcome) variable here, accompanied by units: Reaction Time (ms).
  • Axis Limits: Avoid arbitrary axis truncations unless scientifically warranted, and explicitly signal axis breaks.
  • Regression Fit Line: When illustrating a functional trend, include the least-squares line along with a translucent 95% confidence interval band.

3. Colorblind accessibility (Viridis & ColorBrewer)

Up to 8% of men and 0.5% of women have color vision deficiency (most commonly deuteranomaly or protanomaly, red-green blindness). Avoid using simple red and green dots to distinguish experimental groups. Employ perceptually uniform color maps such as Viridis or ColorBrewer palettes (e.g., navy blue vs. amber orange), and vary marker shapes (circles, squares, triangles) so that figures remain readable in grayscale print.

4. Formatting in APA 7th Edition

In APA style, figure elements follow a structured hierarchy:

  1. Figure Number: Bold font above the chart (e.g., Figure 1).
  2. Figure Title: Title case in italics beneath the number (e.g., Scatter Plot of Training Hours and Cognitive Score).
  3. Note below the figure: Begins with Note. in italics, explaining abbreviations, sample sizes, and trendline equations.
