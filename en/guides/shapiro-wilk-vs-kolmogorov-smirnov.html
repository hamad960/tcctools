Shapiro-Wilk vs Kolmogorov-Smirnov: which test to trust

Updated October 1, 2026 · 11 min read · By tcctools Editorial Team

Before running parametric hypothesis tests such as an independent t-test or ANOVA, researchers must evaluate whether data conform to a normal distribution. In statistical packages like SPSS, two test outputs appear side by side: Shapiro-Wilk and Kolmogorov-Smirnov. When they conflict, which one should you trust and defend in your dissertation?

1. Direct verdict: Shapiro-Wilk is superior

If you need an unequivocal, empirically backed recommendation: rely on the Shapiro-Wilk test.

Systematic power analyses (e.g., Razali & Wah, 2011; Yap & Sim, 2011) demonstrate that Shapiro-Wilk provides the highest sensitivity across sample sizes ranging from \(n = 7\) to \(n = 2000\), comfortably outperforming Kolmogorov-Smirnov, Anderson-Darling, and Jarque-Bera.

2. Why Kolmogorov-Smirnov falls short

  • Parameter Estimation Problem: The original K-S test requires knowing true population parameters a priori. In research, parameters are estimated from sample data. The Lilliefors correction remedies this bias but still leaves K-S with significantly less statistical power than Shapiro-Wilk.
  • Insensitivity in the Tails: K-S measures only the maximum vertical distance between empirical and theoretical cumulative distribution functions. Because cumulative distributions approach 0 and 1 at the extremes, K-S is relatively insensitive to tail deviations—precisely where non-normality most harms t-tests and regression models.

3. Sample size dynamics

Sample Size Test Behavior Recommended Course of Action
Small (n < 20) Low power. Often yields p > .05 despite heavy non-normality. Rely primarily on Q-Q plots; default to non-parametric tests if ambiguous.
Moderate (20 ≤ n ≤ 200) Optimal operating range for Shapiro-Wilk. Trust Shapiro-Wilk p-value corroborated by skewness and kurtosis.
Large (n > 300) Hypersensitive: rejects normality on trivial imperfections. Rely on Central Limit Theorem robustness and visual Q-Q linearity.

4. Methodology chapter write-up template

Example methodology statement:

"Data normality was evaluated using the Shapiro-Wilk test, favored over Kolmogorov-Smirnov due to its superior power across moderate sample sizes (Razali & Wah, 2011; Shapiro & Wilk, 1965). Statistical tests were supplemented with visual inspection of normal Q-Q plots and skewness/kurtosis coefficients."
