Cohen's d effect size: how to calculate and interpret it

Updated October 1, 2026 · 9 min read · By tcctools Editorial Team

In empirical research, finding a statistically significant difference (\(p < .05\)) between groups only tells half the story. It indicates that the observed difference is unlikely due to random sampling error alone, but fails to convey the practical magnitude of the finding. Cohen’s d bridges this gap by quantifying effect magnitude in standardized units.

1. When to use Cohen's d

Cohen's d is the gold standard effect size measure when comparing the means of two continuous groups (e.g., in independent or paired t-tests). It expresses the gap between two sample means in terms of pooled standard deviations. A value of \(d = 0.50\) denotes that the experimental group's mean is half a standard deviation higher than the control group's mean.

2. Formula

For two independent groups, Cohen's d divides the raw mean difference by the pooled standard deviation (\(s_p\)):

d = (M₁ − M₂) / s_p

Where the pooled standard deviation weights each group's variance by its degrees of freedom:

s_p = √[ ((n₁ − 1)·s₁² + (n₂ − 1)·s₂²) / (n₁ + n₂ − 2) ]

3. Interpretation benchmarks (Cohen, 1988)

|d| Value Magnitude Practical Interpretation
< 0.20NegligibleHardly noticeable in clinical or field settings.
0.20 to 0.49SmallSubtle; requires careful measurement to isolate.
0.50 to 0.79MediumNoticeable to a trained observer.
0.80 to 1.19LargeSubstantial and of evident practical importance.
≥ 1.20Very LargeStriking separation between distributions.

4. Cohen's d vs. Hedges' g

In smaller samples (\(n < 20\) per group), Cohen’s d slightly overestimates the population parameter. Larry Hedges introduced an unbiased estimator known as Hedges' g, which applies a sample size correction factor:

g = d × [ 1 − (3 / (4·(n₁ + n₂) − 9)) ]

With samples exceeding 50 participants per condition, the difference between d and g is practically trivial.

5. APA 7th Edition reporting format

Example write-up:

"Participants in the intervention group scored significantly higher (M = 78.4, SD = 10.2) than those in the control group (M = 69.1, SD = 11.5), t(58) = 3.31, p = .002. The effect size was large, Cohen's d = 0.86, 95% CI [0.33, 1.38], indicating substantial real-world efficacy."
