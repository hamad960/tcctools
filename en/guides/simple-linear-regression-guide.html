Simple linear regression: a step-by-step guide

Updated October 1, 2026 · 11 min read · By tcctools Editorial Team

While correlation quantifies how two variables move together symmetrically, simple linear regression models a directional relationship. It allows you to predict the value of an outcome (Y) based on a predictor (X) and test whether that prediction is statistically meaningful.

1. The fundamental regression equation

Y = β₀ + β₁·X + ε
  • Y (Dependent / Outcome variable): The variable you seek to predict.
  • X (Independent / Predictor variable): The variable hypothesized to influence Y.
  • \(\beta_0\) (Intercept): The expected value of Y when X equals zero.
  • \(\beta_1\) (Slope): The average change in Y for each one-unit increase in X.
  • \(\varepsilon\) (Residual error): The vertical discrepancy between observed data points and the fitted regression line.

2. The LINE assumptions

Assumption Definition Diagnostic Method
Linearity Mean values of Y follow a straight-line function of X. Scatter plot of X vs Y; residual plot vs fitted values.
Independence Observation errors are uncorrelated across subjects. Sound experimental design; Durbin-Watson for sequential data.
Normality Model residuals follow a normal distribution. Shapiro-Wilk test on residual series; normal Q-Q plot.
Equal Variance Residual variance remains constant across all values of X (homoscedasticity). Fitted vs residual scatter plot showing even horizontal band without funnel shape.

3. Interpreting R-squared (R²)

The coefficient of determination (\(R^2\)) ranges from 0 to 1. An \(R^2 = .44\) indicates that 44% of the variance in the outcome variable is explained by the regression equation. The remaining 56% is attributed to unmeasured extraneous factors or random noise.

4. APA 7th Edition write-up template

Example reporting paragraph:

"A simple linear regression was calculated to predict employee productivity scores based on training hours completed. A significant regression equation was found, F(1, 48) = 38.14, p < .001, with an R² of .44. Participants' predicted productivity score increased by 2.45 points for each training hour completed (B = 2.45, SE = 0.40, t = 6.18, p < .001, 95% CI [1.65, 3.25]). Residuals conformed to normality (Shapiro-Wilk p = .41) and homoscedasticity."
