How to interpret a 95% confidence interval

Updated October 1, 2026 · 9 min read · By tcctools Editorial Team

Journal editors, peer reviewers, and academic committees increasingly demand that authors present 95% Confidence Intervals (95% CI) instead of solely relying on p-values. While a p-value answers a binary question (whether to reject the null hypothesis), a confidence interval conveys both the magnitude of an effect and the precision of the measurement.

1. The common textbook misconception

Incorrect definition often penalized in defense:

"There is a 95% probability that the true population mean lies between 12.4 and 18.2."

In frequentist statistics, the population parameter is a fixed, unchanging value. Once an interval is calculated from observed sample data, the parameter is either inside it or not: the probability is strictly 1 or 0.

The correct interpretation:

"We are 95% confident that the interval [12.4, 18.2] contains the true population mean, in the sense that if we repeated the experiment and calculated confidence intervals across many samples, 95% of those intervals would capture the true parameter."

2. Interval width and precision

  • Narrow Interval: Indicates high precision and certainty. Achieved through robust sample sizes and low measurement error.
  • Wide Interval: Signals heavy estimation uncertainty. Even when an effect is statistically significant (\(p < .05\)), an overly broad interval (e.g., recovery time reduction between 0.1 and 18.5 hours) suggests the study was underpowered to estimate the true magnitude reliably.

3. The null value benchmark (Zero)

When reporting differences between conditions:

  • If the 95% CI excludes zero (e.g., [2.1, 7.4]), the difference is statistically significant at \(\alpha = .05\).
  • If the 95% CI straddles zero (e.g., [−1.2, 4.8]), the null hypothesis cannot be rejected at \(\alpha = .05\).

4. APA 7th Edition reporting format

Example APA statement:

"Participants demonstrated a significant reduction in depressive symptoms following the intervention, M_diff = 6.30, 95% CI [3.20, 9.40], t(44) = 4.12, p < .001."
